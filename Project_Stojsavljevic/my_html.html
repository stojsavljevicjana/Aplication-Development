Nobel pen shells

Welcome to the WebGIS applicaation project on the topic nobel pen shells. This page is a project in the course: Application development in the summer semestar in the University of Salzburg.

On this project you can found about nobel pen shells, their location in Croatian coasts and their importance.

"Nobel pen shell (Pinna Nobilis) is the second largest bivalve in the world and the largest in the Mediterranean. The cultural and ecological importance of this bivalve reaches back to ancient times, but in the present day they teeter on the brink of extinction. They are a remarkable sight, often growing to 50 cm and sometimes as tall as a meter. Their fan shaped shell is surprisingly thin and hides a beautiful, iridescent, pearl-colored interior. But perhaps their most remarkable feature isn’t theirs at all, but the many tiny plants and animals that call them home." TAKEN FROM:HERE!

Extinction

The massive extinction of the nobel pen shells started in 2016. The grate extinction started at the costs of Spain. The main reason for the extinction was pollution, habitat loss and climate change. Researchers that was working on the problem of the extinction believed that the main couse of the sudden deaths was the Haplosporidium pinnae, a pathogenic microorganism that affects a mollusk’s digestive system. As they got deep in the research they figure out that the problem is more deeper and complex than it was firstly presented. Deeper factors was bacteria, viruses and external issues including sea temperature and environmental change. TAKEN FROM:HERE! Picture bellow shows the dead nobel penn shell.

Saveing the shells

Scientist in the world are trying to come up with new methods and ideas for the perserving this shells. But, all people in the world should HELP with perserving this beautiful creatures. People should not pick them up if they see them or mess with them. People should not polute water more. WARNING: DO NOT PICK SHELLS AND KEEP OUR OCEANS, SEAS, RIVERS CLEAN.
